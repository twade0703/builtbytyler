Straight answers

The questions that come up on every call.

Do I own the site, or am I renting it?

You own it. The domain is registered in your name, the code is yours, and the care plan is a service you can cancel. Nothing is held hostage — that is the single biggest difference between this and the template subscriptions that dominate the small-business market.

Why is there a monthly fee at all?

Because a site that is never touched again quietly rots: dependencies go out of date, security headers drift, forms break when a provider changes an API, and content goes stale. The plan covers hosting, backups, monitoring, updates and small changes. If you would rather host it yourself and take that on, you can — the build price is the same.

Can you work with the site I already have?

Sometimes. If it is on a platform that lets me at the code, yes. If it is locked inside a builder that will not export, rebuilding is usually cheaper than fighting it, and I will tell you which of the two you are looking at on the first call.

How do you handle payments and customer data?

Payments run through Stripe on their own hosted checkout — card details are never entered on, stored on, or transmitted through a site I build. Customer data stays in your accounts, under your ownership, with access I can be removed from.

You are one person. What happens if you disappear?

A fair question and the reason everything is handed over: the repository, the deployment configuration and written documentation are yours from launch. Any competent developer can pick it up, because it is standard code rather than a proprietary system only I can operate.

What do you need from me to start?

Twenty minutes on a call, whatever you already have in the way of logos, photos and copy, and one person who can make decisions. That is genuinely it — the writing and the structure are my job, not homework I hand back to you.
