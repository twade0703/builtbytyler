AI Receptionist terms

BuiltByTyler LLC · AI Receptionist

Version 1, 4 November 2026.

Who you're dealing with

BuiltByTyler LLC, a California LLC in Monterey. When I say "I" below, that means the company. I'm Tyler Wade. I own it, I do the work, I sign for it.

What it does

The AI Receptionist is an AI that answers your business's phone. I give you a phone number of its own, and you set your phone to forward the calls you don't pick up to that number. On each call it:

  • greets the caller in your business's name and tells them it's an AI assistant;
  • asks what they need, then a few follow-up questions: the ones a good dispatcher would ask about common trade problems (a leak, no heat, a tripped breaker), or the questions you've given me;
  • answers their questions from your website and the notes you give me;
  • offers open times from your calendar, and if they pick one, takes their name and address and puts the appointment on your calendar;
  • if they'd rather talk to a person, takes a message for a callback, or puts them through to your phone if you've asked me to turn that on;
  • emails you the details of every booking and every callback request.

If a caller mentions a gas smell, sparks, smoke or fire, it tells them to get out and call 911 or the utility, and emails you straight away.

What it doesn't do, and what I don't promise

  • It's an AI, not a person, and it never claims to be one. Ask it and it says so.
  • It won't answer every call. Calls you don't forward never reach it, some callers hang up, and phones and computers sometimes fail. When it can't pick up, the call rings the phone number you gave me instead, so the caller reaches you, not a dead line.
  • It can pick up at any hour a call is forwarded to it, but I don't promise it's up every minute of every day.
  • It doesn't make up prices or diagnose anything. It tells callers the price comes from you once you've seen the job. If you've written a price on your website or in your notes, it may repeat that, exactly as written.
  • It doesn't promise anything on your behalf. Gate codes, pets, "call before you come": it writes them in the notes for you.
  • No results promised. I don't promise more calls, more customers or more jobs, and it doesn't replace you or your staff. Whether a booking turns into a job is between you and your caller.
  • It isn't built for medical, legal or financial calls, or any calls where the law gives callers' details special protection. Please don't use it for those.

Texts

Texts to your callers (a booking confirmation, or a "sorry we missed you" text when someone hangs up) and texts to you stay off until your business has its own texting registration with the phone carriers. I'll tell you what that takes before anything is set up. Until then, you get emails and your callers don't get texts.

No form, no phone call, no fee, no notice period. It keeps answering through the month you've paid for. No partial refunds, nothing further charged.

Switch your call forwarding off before that month ends. After it ends, the number stops answering.

The number is on my phone account. If you want to keep it when you leave, tell me before your last month ends and I'll try to move it over to you. That depends on the phone companies, so I can't promise it.

Your callers, and what it keeps

For each call, it keeps:

  • the caller's phone number, and when the call started and ended;
  • a written transcript: what the caller said, as the speech-to-text heard it, and what it said back;
  • what the caller told it: their name, address, what they need, their answers to your questions, and any booking or callback;
  • a two- or three-sentence summary the AI writes after the call;
  • any text messages sent to or from the number.

I don't record the audio of calls. Only the words are kept, as text.

To work, the call passes through a few companies, each only for its part:

  • Twilio runs the phone number and carries the call and any texts.
  • Deepgram turns the caller's speech into words, and ElevenLabs turns the AI's words into its voice. Both run inside Twilio's service.
  • Anthropic (the company behind Claude) reads the conversation so the AI can understand the caller and answer, and writes the summary.
  • Google holds your calendar and sends your email notices, from BuiltByTyler's Google account.
  • OpenStreetMap's public map services get the address a caller gives, to check it's real and how far it is from your base, if you've given me a base address.

The call records themselves are stored on a computer I run.

What I do with it

Your callers' details are yours, not mine. I use them for one thing: running your receptionist and fixing it when it goes wrong. I don't sell them, don't add anyone to a list, don't contact your callers about anything of mine, and don't use them to train anything. I hold them to the same standard California law would hold you to if you held them yourself.

Ask me any time what I hold, where it is and who else touches it, and I'll tell you within a week. If anything changes that stops me meeting this, I'll tell you before it affects your data, not after.

How long it's kept

While you're a customer, I keep your call records so either of us can look back at a call. Ask and I'll send you any transcript.

  • Word-for-word transcripts and the technical log I use for fixing problems are deleted after 90 days.
  • After a year, a call keeps only its date and how it ended. The caller's number, name, address, what they needed and the summary are wiped.
  • Bookings stay as your job records while you're a customer. They're on your calendar too.
  • When your last paid month ends, I delete all your call records, bookings and texts within 30 days.
  • Ask any time and I'll delete one call, everything from one caller, or all of it, sooner.

The one thing I keep: if a caller texts STOP, I keep that text, so they're never texted again.

Your calendar

If you want it to offer times and book them, you share your Google Calendar with my Google account and let it make changes. That lets it:

  • check when you're busy before it offers a time. It reads the times of your events, and I can see their titles while I'm setting things up or fixing something;
  • add the appointments it books, with the caller's name, number, address and what they need;
  • move or remove only the appointments it added. It never changes or deletes an event you made.

If it can't read your calendar (Google is down, or you've stopped sharing), it doesn't offer times. It takes the caller's details for a callback instead and emails you.

It can still get a time wrong, for example if you added something a minute ago. The email for each booking is your check. You can stop sharing any time in Google Calendar.

Telling your callers

Every call starts with it saying it's an AI assistant. You can change the rest of the greeting, but not that part. If a caller asks whether they're talking to a real person, it tells them the truth. If they ask whether the call is recorded, it tells them there's no audio recording and that it keeps written notes.

Your side

  • Tell me what it should know: your hours, services, questions and anything callers ask about, and tell me when they change. It answers from what you give it and from your website, so if those are wrong, its answers will be too.
  • Call back the people who ask for it. It takes the message; the job is yours.
  • Keep the email address you gave me working. That's where every booking and callback goes.
