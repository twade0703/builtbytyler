The short version

I have been taking things apart and putting them back together wrong, then right, for as long as I can remember. Everything here started that way.

The mechanical side came first: parts analysed before they get cut and tested after they are assembled, iterated until they hold up under load rather than on a screen. The software side grew out of it, because a drone or a robot is never just mechanical — it is a structure and the control that brings it to life, designed as one system. That intersection is where the interesting problems live.

It is also why I build software for other businesses. Most people making websites have never had to make something work where a failure is a part on the floor rather than a bug report. That changes how you build: you test it, you document it, and you hand it over so somebody else can maintain it. A business website is a small problem by comparison, which is exactly why it should be done properly, quickly, and for a price you can see before you call.

I work out of Monterey, California. If you email me, I am the one who answers.

How I work

Four things I will not trade away.

Every one of these costs me something. They are here because the alternative costs you more, and because a promise you can check is worth more than a page of adjectives.

  • Everything ships with its source CAD, firmware, code and documentation. If you paid for it, you own it outright and you can take it anywhere.
  • Nothing is bought and rebadged No white-labelled hardware, no page-builder templates. It is designed here or it is not sold here.
  • You deal with me No account manager, no handoff to a junior. The person who scopes the work is the person who builds it, and the person who answers the email.
  • I will talk you out of it If a build or a piece of software is the wrong answer for what you are trying to do, I would rather say so on the first call than take the money.
